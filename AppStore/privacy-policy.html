Dropwall — Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 October 2026

Dropwall is made by KERN AG, Germany. Questions about this policy go to hottorun@pm.me.

The short version

You do not need an account to use Dropwall. You never give us a name, an email address or a password. If you choose to sign in with Apple — so that your follows, sizes and alerts survive a reinstall, or to add profiles for other people — we receive an identifier Apple makes for Dropwall alone, and nothing else about you: not your name and not your email address. We do not ask Apple for either.

Almost everything the app knows about you stays on your phone. The exception is the small amount of information our server needs in order to tell you that something you are waiting for has been released, has come back in stock or has been marked down, and the searches you ask it to run. When you use one of the features that ask AI — asking Dropwall in Shop, scanning a garment, asking about a fit — what you asked about goes through our server to Anthropic, the company that makes the Claude model, to be answered; see AI features below. Nothing is sent to it unless you ask.

We do not sell data, we show no advertising, we use no third-party analytics or attribution services, and we do not track you across other companies' apps or websites. The one thing we count is how often the app's main features are used, as anonymous daily totals on our own server — see Anonymous usage counts below.

What stays on your device and is never sent to us

  • Everything you save, and the notes, sizes and photographs attached to it — including the photographs you take of your own clothes, which are kept on the phone. One is sent only when you ask for it to be read or judged (see AI features)
  • Your boards and the outfits you compose
  • Your daily picks, and anything you mark "not for me" — apart from the short description described under AI features
  • Anything you write in the style statement field
  • The taste profile used to recommend brands and pick products, and any brand you have dismissed
  • Any release date you enter yourself in the drop calendar, along with its title and note
  • The analysis of saved photographs — colour, shape, category — which is performed entirely on the device using Apple's on-device frameworks, with no image ever uploaded

If you use profiles, each profile's saves, boards, outfits and taste are kept separately on the phone, and none of them is sent to us either.

Brand recommendations and daily picks are computed on your phone: our server sends products and candidate brands, and the comparison against your taste happens locally, specifically so that what you save does not have to leave the device.

Deleting the app deletes all of it. We hold no copy and cannot recover it.

What our server receives

  • An anonymous device record. When the app first runs it registers with our server and is issued a random identifier. Unless you sign in, it is not linked to you or to your Apple Account.
  • A push notification token, if you allow notifications, so that Apple can deliver alerts to your device. Removed if Apple tells us the token is no longer valid.
  • Which brands you follow, and for each whether you want everything, only sales and price drops, or nothing at all, so we know which storefronts to watch and what to tell you.
  • Which products you have asked to be alerted about, including the size or colour you asked for.
  • What you type into Shop's search box, so our server can search everything the shops you follow sell. It is used to answer that one search and then discarded: it is not stored, not written to our logs, and not linked to you or your device. Finding products that look alike runs on our own server too — no search, and no product, is sent to another company to do it.
  • Your credit balance and purchases, if you are signed in: each credit pack you buy (the App Store's transaction, which names the pack and when it was bought, never a payment detail — Apple handles the payment), each credit spent, with the kind of action it paid for and when, and what each AI action cost us. These are held so your balance survives a reinstall and a refund can be applied. They are linked to your account.
  • Your size preferences and your gender filter. These are held so that a restock alert in a size you do not wear is never sent to you. That targeting is the only reason we have them.
  • The country you shop in — your phone's region, unless you choose another under You › Location. It is held so that, where a brand runs a separate shop for your country, your feed and alerts come from that shop and its prices. It is a country and nothing finer: never your location, your address or your phone's position.
  • A brand and a time, when you enter a release date yourself and are following that brand, so our server can check that storefront more often around that moment. Only the brand and the instant are sent — never the title or the note you wrote, and never any date for a brand you do not follow. Nobody else can read it, and no notification or public record is produced from it.
  • Anonymous usage counts — see the next section.
  • Ordinary server logs, including IP addresses, kept briefly for security and debugging.

If you sign in with Apple

Signing in is optional, and everything above works without it.

  • An identifier from Apple. Apple gives each app a different identifier for the same person, so ours means nothing outside Dropwall. We use it to recognise your account when you sign in on a new phone or after a reinstall.
  • A token from Apple that lets us cancel Dropwall's sign-in with Apple when you delete your account. We use it for nothing else, and it is never shown to anyone.
  • Your device records, follows, alerts, sizes and country are linked to the account, so that they come back when you sign in again. Signing in on a phone that already had its own follows combines the two.

If you add profiles

A profile is for another person you shop for — "For Anna". For each profile, our server holds the name you give it, and that profile's own follows, alerts, sizes, gender filter and whether its alerts are switched on. The name is printed at the start of that profile's alerts ("For Anna: …"), so it passes through Apple's notification service like the rest of the alert. Use any name you like; it does not have to be a real one.

AI features

Some features are answered by Claude, a model made by Anthropic. Each one runs only when you ask for it, costs credits (or is free and says so), and sends only what that answer needs. Anthropic receives it from our server, not from your phone, so it never sees your device, your account or your IP address. Anthropic processes it on our behalf to answer the request, does not use it to train its models, and deletes it after the limited period its commercial terms allow. On our own server none of it is stored or written to our logs; we keep only the numbers needed for billing — which kind of action, which model, how much text it took and what it cost.

  • Asking Dropwall in Shop: the description you typed and your short replies in that search. "Show me more" also sends your country, so the shops it finds sell where you live.
  • Scanning a garment: the one photograph you chose, after the phone has cut the garment out where it can. Deciding whether it goes with your wardrobe happens on the phone; your wardrobe is not sent.
  • Asking about a fit: the pieces in it — their titles, colours and photographs (a shop's photograph by its address, or your own photograph if the piece is one you scanned) — and, when you ask for suggestions, the candidates the phone chose.
  • Daily picks: so a pick's reason can be written in one line, the pick and a short description of the piece of yours it goes with — "black chore jacket" — are sent: never its photograph, its brand or anything else about it. The sentence is kept on our server for up to thirty days with no device or account attached, so anyone with a similar pick gets the same sentence.

Our server also asks Claude to read shops' own pages — the small print of a sale banner, or a product page that publishes nothing machines can read. That is about the shop, never about you.

Anonymous usage counts

So that we can tell which parts of the app people actually use, the app counts a handful of things, per day:

  • opening the app
  • opening each of its tabs (Feed, Shop, Saved)
  • running a brand search
  • asking us to check a shop nobody has added yet
  • following a brand
  • saving something
  • creating a fit
  • tapping one of our notifications

It sends only how many times each of those happened on each day. Never what you searched for, saved, followed or looked at; never which product or brand; never a time finer than the date.

Our server adds those numbers to totals shared by everyone who uses the app, and keeps nothing else from what was sent: there is no record of which device sent which counts. Like everything else the app sends us, the counts arrive with your device's anonymous credential — that is how we stop anyone flooding the totals — and they are separated from it on arrival.

To count how many people use the app in a week without counting one person twice, your device record holds one extra fact: the date (not the time) on which it last sent counts. It is replaced each day rather than added to, so it is never a history.

The counts go to our server and nowhere else. No analytics company, SDK or third party is involved. If the app is running without our server, nothing is counted or sent.

Where product photographs come from

Product photographs and brand logos are loaded by your device directly from each brand's own website or content delivery network, rather than being copied through our server. This is normal for any app that displays images from the web, and it means those companies can see your device's IP address and the fact that an image was requested — the same as if you had opened the brand's website in a browser. They receive nothing from us about you.

The same is true when you check a product's stock on its page, open a brand's checkout, or share a link into the app: your phone asks that website directly.

Who else is involved

  • Apple, which delivers push notifications and, if you use it, provides Sign in with Apple. Apple's own privacy policy applies to both.
  • Anthropic, which answers the AI features you ask for, as described under AI features, and processes what it receives only to answer it.
  • Our hosting provider, which runs the server and its database on our behalf and does not use the data for anything else.

That is the complete list. There is no advertising network, no third-party analytics service and no data broker of any kind in this app.

App Tracking Transparency

The app does not track you in Apple's sense of the word: nothing here is joined with data collected by other companies' apps or websites, and nothing is shared with a data broker. That is why you are never shown the tracking permission prompt.

How long we keep things

  • Release history older than thirty days is deleted from our server, along with products that no storefront has listed for six months.
  • A release date you sent as a polling hint is deleted shortly after that date has passed.
  • Your device record, your follows, your alerts, your size preferences and your country are kept for as long as you use the app.
  • If you signed in, your account and its profiles are kept until you delete the account, and with them your credit balance and its history.
  • What each AI action cost us is kept as numbers only — no text, no photograph — to set the price of credits. Deleting your account removes the account from those numbers; what is left describes nobody.
  • The usage totals are kept indefinitely, because they describe nobody. The date your device last sent counts is replaced each day.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes everything held on the device immediately.

If you signed in, you can delete your account in the app, under You › Account. That deletes, on our server, the account, every profile on it, every device record signed in to it, and their follows, alerts, sizes, countries and release dates, and its credit balance and history, and cancels Dropwall's sign-in with Apple. Unspent credits are lost with the account. Deleting a single profile deletes that profile's data the same way. Your phone keeps its own saves and carries on without an account.

If you never signed in, write to hottorun@pm.me from any address and ask us to delete your server-side record. Because there is no account, please include the device you are asking about so we can identify the right record. We will delete it and confirm.

Children

Dropwall is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask what we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, or to object to our use of it. Write to hottorun@pm.me and we will answer. Given that we hold no name or email address — only the names you give profiles — the practical answer to "what do you hold about me" is the list in What our server receives above.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes we will update the date at the top of this page. Material changes will also be described in the app's release notes.
